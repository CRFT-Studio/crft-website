A wireframe is an early design artifact that maps out the structure of a page or product screen. It prioritizes placement of content, navigation, and key interactions over colors, imagery, and polished styling.

## Purpose of Wireframes

Wireframes help teams align on information architecture and user flows before investing in high-fidelity design or development. They make it easier to discuss hierarchy, content needs, and usability without getting distracted by visual details.

## Types of Wireframes

### Low-Fidelity Wireframes
Simple sketches or grayscale boxes used for rapid exploration. Ideal for workshops, early concepts, and stakeholder alignment.

### Mid-Fidelity Wireframes
More detailed layouts with clearer spacing, labeled components, and basic interaction notes.

### High-Fidelity Wireframes
Near-complete structural designs that may include real content and precise component placement, but still stop short of full visual branding.

## What Wireframes Typically Include

- Page structure and content blocks
- Navigation placement
- Call-to-action locations
- Form fields and key UI components
- Notes on interactions or states
- Content hierarchy and priority

## Wireframes vs Mockups vs Prototypes

| Artifact | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| Wireframe | Structure and layout |
| Mockup | Visual design and branding |
| Prototype | Interaction and flow |

## Benefits

### Faster Feedback
Teams can critique structure early, when changes are cheap.

### Clearer Requirements
Wireframes reduce ambiguity for content, design, and engineering.

### Better Prioritization
They force decisions about what matters most on a page.

## Best Practices

1. Start simple and increase fidelity only when needed
2. Use real or realistic content whenever possible
3. Annotate interactions that are not obvious
4. Validate key flows with stakeholders and users
5. Keep wireframes linked to user goals, not just page inventory

Wireframing is a planning tool, not a final deliverable. Used well, it reduces rework and helps teams ship clearer, more intentional interfaces.
